Outbound messages to people must be actively **de-AI-ified**, not just "clean". Three parts:

1. **No dashes, at all.** Not em dashes (—), not en dashes (–), and not a hyphen used as a connector or in place of a comma/colon. Use commas, periods, or a new sentence. Hyphens inside real words or identifiers are fine.
2. **Short.** See [[feedback_short_messages]]. A few plain lines. If it needs structure, it is too long.
3. **Slightly imperfect, like a person typing.** Perfect capitalisation and punctuation on every line is itself an AI tell. An occasional lowercase sentence start reads human. Don't overdo it, and never introduce errors into names, addresses, commands or identifiers.

Also avoid: triadic lists, "Happy to X", restating their question back, bold-heavy structuring, headings/tables/bullets in chat, "I hope this finds you well".

**Tone by recipient.** Default is neutral colleague-to-colleague. For someone senior to the user, warm it slightly: "do you happen to know", offer an alternative rather than announcing a decision, "whichever you think is quicker". Still short, still no deference or apologising for asking.

**Why:** messages the user sends to colleagues must read as written by them, not by a tool. Obvious AI phrasing undermines credibility, especially with managers. Corrected repeatedly, including a two-message draft that was still too long and still carried dash punctuation, so treat length and dashes as hard rules.

**How to apply:** draft it, cut it in half, strip every dash, then check whether it reads like a colleague thumb-typing on a phone. Only then show it to the user for approval ([[feedback_check_before_sending]]). Internal docs and reports are exempt.
